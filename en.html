02 Macro & Curve Dislocations

Jackson Hole — the problem to solve is inflation, not employment. In his first speech the Chair restated that the 2% PCE target is a firm and fixed objective, and judged it too early to call underlying inflation improved with July PCE at 3.7% y/y and 4.1% annualised over six months. He cited the fact that 54% of PCE components ran above 3% over the past twelve months as evidence the pressure is broad rather than confined to a few items. On the labour side he pointed to 4.1% unemployment and low claims as consistent with full employment, adding that with constrained labour supply a slower monthly payroll pace is the natural outcome.

"More work to do" — the door to another hike is now more clearly open. He gave no timing and no conditions, however, and there is little inflation data to see before the September FOMC, so a September hike is far from settled. Markets read it as hawkish regardless: 2Y +11.3bp, with the front end selling off harder than the long end — a clean bear flattening.

Korea handed back the post-BOK rally. On 08-28 KTB yields rose 2Y +3.7bp (3.665%), 3Y +4.5bp (3.795%), 5Y +5.4bp (4.022%), 10Y +6.4bp (4.285%), 20Y +2.6bp (4.491%) and 30Y +2.7bp (4.527%) — led by the belly out to 10 years. The ultra-long end gave back only half as much, taking 10-30 in by 3.7bp. Futures closed 3Y −0.17 (103.34) and 10Y −0.58 (105.75), driven directly by foreign selling of 25,765 3Y and 16,068 10Y contracts.

Today is about realised demand, not the supply story. Reduced 30Y issuance in the September funding plan and buybacks of off-the-run 20s and 30s have already priced much of the supply relief. What moves the ultra-long end and the 10-30 curve from here is how much month-end WGBI passive money actually arrives — the first thing to check today.

A second day of foreign selling, and more than twice the size — −25,765 in the 3Y and −16,068 in the 10Y. The 3Y cumulative has retraced 36,920 in two sessions, from a six-month high of 133,199 on 8/26 to 96,279. Taking the other side were securities firms (+11,445 3Y, +11,950 10Y) and banks (+8,454). Part of this is position trimming ahead of the weekend and the Chair's speech, so the size alone does not confirm a change of direction.

  • ReviewOn 08-28 the 10Y +6.4bp and 5Y +5.4bp led the sell-off while the ultra-long end rose only 2.7bp. Futures closed 3Y −0.17 (103.34), 10Y −0.58 (105.75), with the 10Y range at 55 ticks and volume down to 88k. The view that the BOK-day rally had gone too far too fast was the stated driver.
  • 3YEst. open 103.21 (13 ticks lower), range 103.09~103.36. The 103.34 close sits at 93% of that range, so the open alone drops it back to mid-range. First support is the weekly low at 103.04.
  • 10YEst. open 105.48 (27 ticks lower), range 105.02~105.91 — 89 ticks wide. This is the first session to trade the speech, and the range carries the volatility premium accordingly.
  • CurveOnly 5-10 (26.3bp, +1.17σ) screens above 1σ in cash. The futures 3-10 at 47.6bp, +1.57σ is materially richer, so a flattener is better expressed in futures than in cash. KR–US 10Y at −43.6bp (Korea lower), 20-day z −1.18.
  • WatchThe first thing to confirm today is how much month-end WGBI passive money lands. Supply relief is already in the price; without real demand the 10-30 can widen back out. Read it alongside tomorrow's 30Y auction.
