02 Macro & Curve Dislocations

Fiscal expansion pushed the ultra-long end up. KTB yields rose 2Y +2.5bp (3.720%), 3Y +3.9bp (3.879%), 5Y +5.7bp (4.110%), 10Y +6.0bp (4.370%), 20Y +6.9bp (4.560%) and 30Y +12.4bp (4.627%) — a bear steepening that grew with maturity. The trigger was the President telling a cabinet meeting that growth above 3% is becoming realistic and that "the rise in rates cannot be avoided", alongside a 2027 budget set at KRW 820.9tn (+93tn, +12.8%) — the largest ever in both size and growth rate. Cutting the other way: next year's KTB issuance is KRW 222.8tn, KRW 2.9tn below this year's original budget.

The reversal flagged yesterday arrived within a session. 10-30 widened 6.4bp to 25.7bp, 5-30 6.7bp to 51.7bp and 20-30 3.4bp to 6.7bp. Yesterday's note argued that 10-30, 5-30 and 3-30 sitting at −2.2σ to −2.3σ on 20 days made this a close-the-flattener zone with the 30Y auction as the pivot; it steepened exactly there, and no KTB curve appears on today's anomaly screen at all.

Auction demand improved on August but is still only just over half. The 26-8 went to foreign accounts (6,432), insurers and pensions (5,291) and ITCs (1,879). Against an estimated KRW 1.36tn of real demand, long-duration buyers took 54%. Part of that ratio comes from supply being 3,000 smaller than last month, but the absolute size also rose by more than 1,000. Set against past auctions where insurers alone absorbed 1.5tn, it still falls short.

Foreign accounts bought the benchmark and sold everything else. Net KTB cash buying was only +1,454, but the composition was +6,432 in 26-8 against −4,810 in 26-3, −2,000 in 23-10, −865 in 24-2 and −822 in 25-11. Selling ran through the off-the-run ultra-longs (24-2, 24-8, 23-7, 25-7, 26-2), making this a switch into the auction issue. In futures the 3Y saw 18,364 contracts sold, cutting the cumulative to 72,080.

Overnight the US traded oil again. A Saudi tanker was struck leaving the Strait of Hormuz and the US carried out further strikes on Iran, reviving closure and supply-disruption fears and taking WTI to 90.22 (+5.20%). With ISM manufacturing prices still elevated and employment holding up in a low-hiring, low-firing mix, Treasuries closed 2Y +5.4bp (4.397%), 10Y +4.7bp (4.799%) and 30Y +3.2bp (5.275%) — a bear flattening, the opposite of Korea. KR–US 10Y narrowed 1.3bp to 42.9bp.
