02 Macro & Curve Dislocations

The WGBI passive money showed up in the print. Foreign investors bought KRW 2.27tn of KTB cash on 08-31, flipping a week that had run at a net sale into a large inflow on the final day of the month. By bucket, >10Y +9,202 and ≤5Y +9,839 — the ultra-long end and the belly were bought together. Yesterday's note framed the question as supply expectation versus realised demand; this settles it on the demand side.

The result was a hard flattening. Yields rose at the front — 2Y +3.0bp (3.695%), 3Y +4.5bp (3.840%), 5Y +3.1bp (4.053%), 10Y +2.5bp (4.310%) — while 20Y fell 2.1bp (4.470%) and 30Y fell 2.4bp (4.503%). That took 10-30 in 4.9bp to 19.3bp, 5-30 in 5.5bp to 45.0bp and 10-20 in 4.6bp to 16.0bp in a single session.

Futures, by contrast, barely moved. The 3Y closed −0.11 (103.23) and the 10Y −0.16 (105.59), with ranges of just 10 and 37 ticks against 20 and 55 the day before. A 10-tick range on a day that saw over KRW 2tn of cash buying is itself the evidence: this was real-money passive demand that never went through the futures market. Foreign futures flows split — 3Y −5,835, 10Y +760 — with banks on the other side at +7,498 in the 3Y.

What was bought at the long end were off-the-runs, not benchmarks. Foreign investors took more than 8,500 across KTBs maturing beyond 2040, against roughly 6,000 at prior month-ends — a clear step up. But on-the-run buying was minimal; the demand spread across off-the-run issues in the same area, and part of it may be a sovereign wealth fund buying 25-9, so calling all of it passive money would overstate the case. Insurers and pension funds have visibly eased off the ultra-long dip-buying they ran from mid-August, with no strip buying evident, which points to yesterday's rally being driven by foreign flows and prop desks trading behind them.

Overnight the US traded oil and geopolitics. Renewed US–Iran military tension pushed Brent back above USD 90, feeding inflation expectations and long-end term premium. Treasuries closed 2Y −0.3bp (4.343%), 10Y +3.1bp (4.752%), 30Y +3.6bp (5.243%) — a clean bear steepening, the opposite of Korea, taking KR–US 10Y to −44.2bp. Since Jackson Hole the market has repriced a September FOMC hike back above 65%, and front-end yields have not retraced the spike.

Today brings the 30Y auction, one session after the ultra-long end richened 2bp-plus. The larger point is that month-end WGBI passive flow is by nature one-off: from today what matters is whether real demand from foreign accounts and domestic insurers persists once that bid is gone.
