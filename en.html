03 Macro & Curve Dislocations

A second day of strength, led this time by the 5Y. Cash KTBs 2Y −4.8 (3.807) · 3Y −6.5 (3.852) · 5Y −8.2 (4.020) · 10Y −7.7 (4.220) · 20Y −6.1 (4.295) · 30Y −6.6 (4.319) · 50Y −6.6. Futures +21 ticks to 102.98 (high 103.03) and +63 to 106.04 (high 106.13). The 10Y is −19.2bp over two sessions and −38 from the 9/15 high of 4.60. 3-10 is 36.8, a 60-day low (z −2.41), 10-30 retraced to 9.9 (+1.1), and the 3Y is 85bp over base. KOSPI fell 5.46% to 6,035 and USD/KRW rose 10 won to 1,345 — risk aversion deepened.

The 26-5 shorts were repaid — KRW 1,213bn on D+1. Borrow went 36,389 → 24,259bn (14.1%), twice what 26-7 (−622) and 26-8 (−635) repaid. The "dilution is neutral, repayment is the catalyst" line from yesterday played out within a day, and about half of the 3Y's −6.5bp is this. What remains, KRW 2.4tn, is already below the pre-auction level of 3.5tn. Going the other way, the old 30Y benchmark 26-2 took on KRW 543bn of new shorts (to 2,770bn) and the old 5Y 26-3 +69bn — shorts are migrating from the front to the long end and the off-the-runs. 25-4 −130bn and 25-10 −165bn were repayments in buyback and basket lines.

Offshore futures turned into covering on day two. They bought 14,312 3Y contracts but open interest fell 7,502 to 473,444; in the 10Y +2,996 with OI −1,207. Unlike the day before (+8,267 new length), yesterday's 3Y strength was someone else's shorts being covered. That is different from July 2024 (fifteen straight days of buying with OI rising) and closer to the 5 Aug 2024 signal, where the day the buying stopped was the low. The KRW 655bn of offshore cash KTB buying (supply file) is big only on paper — the 380bn of 23-2 was matched by banks selling the same amount, so it looks like an account transfer (Meritz). Net of that it is sub-one-year buying (25-6, 25-1) against sales of 24-4, 08-2 and 23-10, and in the ultra-long 26-8 +65bn against 26-2, 24-2 and 25-7 −43bn — no direction. Not read as a reversal of 10/12's −645bn. Meritz expects the rally to hold while offshore keep buying futures; our caveat is that the futures buying turned into covering yesterday.

The US long end fell on CPI eve. 10/13 close 2Y 4.7925 (+0.3) · 5Y 5.003 (−1.5) · 10Y 5.2105 (−3.1) · 30Y 5.558 (−4.0), 2-10 41.8 (−3.3). The 10Y is below its 20-day average (5.248) and looking at the lower Bollinger band (5.166); the 2Y sits on its 20-day (4.79) — the December hike is still priced. Waller ("higher yields are doing part of the tightening") and Goolsbee (a conditional return to cuts if inflation improves) weighed on the long end, while oil turned up intraday on Saudi–Houthi clash risk and kept the front from falling. KR10−US10 is −99bp (−4.7), a 60-day low. CPI consensus is headline 0.6% m/m (3.6–3.7% y/y), core 0.2% (2.5%). In the six October CPIs since 2020, what mattered was where yields came from, and with the US 10Y −7.7 MTD this is a print after a rally: a miss still saw yields rebound that week (the 2025 pattern), a beat gives the 2023 pattern (+12 on the day, +40 on the week).

Only 1Y policy-bank paper and 2–3Y card paper (at MSB levels) held up, and with bank-paper and CP funding rates not coming down there is no incentive to chase spreads tighter (Meritz).
