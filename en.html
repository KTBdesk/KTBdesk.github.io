03 Macro Issues & Curve Dislocations

11/3 — the 30Y awarded at 4.070, short covering then a retracement, and the front end after the RBA. It went to script. KOSPI −3.59% (5,648.79) · USD/KRW 1,341.0 (−2.8), CD 3.35 (+1). 3Y over base 73.7 (−0.3).

KRW 2.0tn (increased) of 30Y awarded at 4.070 — 7bp through the prior close of 4.140; the 25.7% on loan came in as short covering. Insurers and pensions took about KRW 918bn (46%, against 32% in October), more aggressive than last time, while offshore took about 300bn (15%, against more than 27% in October), less. Offshore sold off-the-run ultra-longs the same day (24-8 100bn, 26-2 13), and insurers' and pensions' off-the-run buying was limited to 24-2 and 25-7 at 40bn each and 26-2 at 24 — demand concentrated in the one auction line. In the first 30Y auction after the WGBI passive flow ended, offshore showed up in the benchmark but did not spread to the off-the-runs: ultra-long supply will need fresh real-money demand confirmed each time. The 10Y contract pulled back from 108.18 (11:04) right after the award, and the 30Y went from 4.090 at 11:46 to 4.088 at the close — 1.8bp above the 4.070 award, which worked as the 30Y's yield floor barrier. Borrow in 26-8 fell 60bn on 164bn lent against 224bn repaid (2,046bn), the ratio dropping from 25.70 to 20.07% on the larger outstanding. Add the long-term forward rate (LTFR) kept at 4.3% — with the insurers' liability discount rate not lowered, the valuation burden eases and so does the pressure on them to add ultra-longs, the same direction as a 10-30 steepener.

The RBA hiked to 4.85 and the AU 2Y fell 5.5bp — a 6–3 vote (three for a hold) and guidance that policy "is well placed to respond". Not the literal "a pause may be appropriate" written about in the morning, but the vote and the guidance did the same job, and Korea's front end led too — the 2Y −2.0, settling below the 3.725 award. The 3Y, though, was the one tenor that did not rally (−0.3): borrow in 26-5 rose 210bn (1,553bn · 8.67%) as the short started building into the 11/9 3Y auction (26-5 1.1tn + the new 26-12 1.1tn), and offshore 3Y futures went to −133 (net 168,982), ending nearly ten days of buying. Hence 2-3 4.2 (+1.7) our way, while 3-5 13.0 (−2.7) · 3-10 26.3 (−3.7) · 5-10 13.3 (−1.0) all set new 60-day lows — flat on both sides of the 3Y. Borrow in the 2Y benchmark 26-7 fell 303bn on 323bn of repayments (91bn · 0.95%): the short built into the auction was almost entirely covered within two days — the flow input for a 2Y give-back is spent.

In the US yields backed up 4–6bp on JOLTS. 11/3 2Y 4.6309 (+4.2) · 5Y 4.7623 (+6.3) · 10Y 4.9621 (+6.0) · 20Y 5.3606 (+5.4) · 30Y 5.3265 (+5.1), 2-10 33.1 (+1.7) · 10-30 36.4 (−0.9) — a bear steepening with the 5Y weakest. September openings of 7.523M (from 7.19M) beat forecasts by a wide margin, a sign of labour demand holding up, and trimmed Fed easing expectations. Oil stayed pressed by hopes for Russia–Ukraine and US–Iran talks, WTI 96.09 (−0.4%), so the move was jobs and supply (Oracle and Meta funding), not oil. The midterms are counted during the Korean day — the Democrats are favoured and the question is how far the Senate is reshaped. Some read the historical pattern (S&P 500 up 5.7% on average over three months and 12.4% over six after the 13 midterms since 1974) as a warning of higher yields once the uncertainty clears; this time a Democratic Congress under President Trump would be a divided government that constrains further tax cuts and spending, which if anything lowers the deficit and supply worry — so higher yields after the vote are not the default.
