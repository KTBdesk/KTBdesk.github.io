02 Macro & Curve Dislocations

A morning rally handed back in the afternoon. Equities and bonds moved together — both firmed in the morning, then gave up most of the gain as geopolitical risk and crude reasserted themselves after lunch. Futures closed 3Y −1 tick (103.11) and 10Y −4 ticks (105.13), but the 10Y traded as high as 105.52 before settling on its low of 105.13, surrendering the entire back half of a 39-tick range. KOSPI finished at 6,954 (−0.58%).

Crude is a live catalyst again. US–Iran military exchanges and concern over reduced transit through the Strait of Hormuz, together with Houthi attacks on Saudi energy facilities, lifted the Middle East supply-disruption premium another notch. WTI traded above $94 during the Korean session and settled at 93.03 (+0.57%), an eighth consecutive gain. The won weakened to 1,345.60 (+0.38%); the yen was flat at 154.12.

The 10Y and the 20Y went in opposite directions. 10Y +1.0bp (4.400%) underperformed for a second day while 20Y −1.6bp (4.561%) rallied alone, with 2Y −0.6bp (3.715%), 3Y +0.1bp (3.898%), 5Y +0.2bp (4.127%) and 30Y +0.1bp (4.640%) going nowhere. The result: 10-20 narrowed 2.6bp to 16.1, a 5- and 60-day low, while 20-30 widened 1.7bp to 7.9, +1.31σ and the 98.0th percentile of 250 days. One bond moved both spreads — and it did so the day before today's KRW 0.6tn 20Y auction, which is the unusual part.

Offshore sold KRW 459.1bn of cash KTBs, and the selling is all in the very short end. By bucket, ≤1Y at −514.0bn is effectively the whole number — concentrated in FX-stabilisation bond 25-11 (Nov 26) −329.0bn and KTB 16-8 (Dec 26) −155.0bn, an extension of the short-paper selling that has run since August. Buying, by contrast, sat in 2–3Y: 26-1 +118.0bn, 26-5 +62.7bn, 25-10 +49.6bn. Around the 10Y they also trimmed 25-5 −80.0bn and 25-11 −73.8bn. In futures they bought again — 3Y +4,556, 10Y +1,376, taking the cumulative 3Y to 70,203.

Four benchmarks roll tomorrow — and much of the ultra-long activity is that switch, not direction. On 9/10 the 2Y (26-1 → 26-7), 5Y (26-3 → 04125-310), 20Y (25-9 → 26-10) and 30Y (26-2 → 26-8) all change at once, and the 20Y rolls only once a year, so this is the only one in 2026. Through that lens yesterday's flows read differently — offshore selling 26-2 (old 30Y) −10.2bn and 25-9 (old 20Y) −34.8bn while buying 26-8 (new 30Y) +9.0bn, and banks switching 26-8 +178.1bn / 26-2 −258.1bn the session before, are textbook old-into-new benchmark rotation.

Insurers and pension funds are doing the opposite — buying the old benchmark as it cheapens. The same day they took 26-2 +88.0bn (plus 24-2 +50.0bn and 26-8 principal strips +30.0bn, against 22-2 −50.0bn, 26-10 −10.0bn, 21-2 −10.0bn). A bond losing benchmark status loses its liquidity premium and cheapens, and buy-and-hold accounts are picking it up. So the split is offshore and banks rotating into the new lines, insurers and pension funds taking the cheapened old ones — not a directional signal. What does matter: offshore bought only +9.0bn of the new 30Y, so even the rotation demand is lukewarm.

Today — expect a firm morning and a softer close, so keep the holding period short. It is also the day before four benchmarks roll at once, which adds a rally bias of its own: pre-positioning in the incoming lines and position clean-up by index-following accounts land in the same session. Downward pressure on yields should be strong early, but the first resistance sits around 2bp through yesterday's close, with the range top as the second. Selling pressure is likely to build into the close, which argues for trading levels on a short leash rather than holding. At the front end, call at 3.090% (+3.0bp) and the KTB-MSB 2Y spread at −8.5bp (−2.1, a 60-day low) show the funding and MSB supply burden is already in the price, so how the front end trades out of the KRW 2.0tn MSB 2Y auction at 10:00 is the first hurdle of the day.

What that means for the roll — 25-4, which had been holding September expensive, is resolved, and the force keeping December cheap lost more than half its size intraday today. With both causes disappearing together, the roll edge will most likely keep shrinking. There is one way back: if 26-5 stays cheap after today's MSB 2Y auction, December gets pushed down again and the 3Y roll reopens.

Tomorrow's benchmark roll lands directly on the basket — 26-3 loses 5Y benchmark status (to 04125-310). Losing that status should make 26-3 cheaper, and 26-3 sits in both the September and December baskets. → Both fair values get pulled down together, which widens the futures premium.
