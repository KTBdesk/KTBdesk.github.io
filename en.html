02 Macro & Curve Dislocations

Waller took a notch out of the tightening fear. Governor Waller said he could argue for a hold at the September FOMC if coming data show inflation pressure easing, and the market-implied probability of a September hike fell from around 63% to roughly 50%. It is the first meaningful unwind of the hawkish pricing that had built up since Jackson Hole.

Korea rallied across the curve, led by the front. Yields fell 2Y −5.5bp (3.705%), 3Y −5.3bp (3.882%), 5Y −6.2bp (4.108%), 10Y −5.0bp (4.367%), 20Y −3.2bp (4.590%) and 30Y −3.0bp (4.637%) — a bull steepening driven by the front end and belly that had been the drag all week. 10-30 widened 2.0bp to 27.0bp, 5-30 3.2bp to 52.9bp and 10-20 1.8bp to 22.3bp, while only the very front compressed: 1-2 −4.7bp, 1-3 −4.8bp.

Foreign accounts bought the 3Y for the first time in six sessions. They took +9,209 contracts after dumping 66,680 over the previous five. Futures closed 3Y +0.19 (103.14) and 10Y +0.45 (105.20). The 10Y saw just −74 contracts, so the reversal, like the selling before it, is concentrated in the 3Y.

The yen moved. USD/JPY fell 1.98% to 156.65, more than three yen in a session — the same direction as the presumed intervention flagged in yesterday's colour, and the same shape as the bull steepening that followed the early-August intervention. The won firmed alongside it at 1,359.30 (−0.69%).

Tonight is the test. With the labour market back alongside inflation as a variable the Fed is watching, a firm US August payrolls print would rebuild the September hike pricing that just came out. Treasuries closed 2Y −3.1bp (4.339%), 10Y −1.1bp (4.769%) and 30Y −1.0bp (5.249%) — a second day of gains, but again a small one.
