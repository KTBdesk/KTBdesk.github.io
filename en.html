02 Macro & Curve Dislocations

A dovish hike — yields fell across the curve anyway. On 08-27 the BOK raised the base rate from 2.75% to 3.00%, but the market read the accompanying tone as softer than expected and bought. KTB cash richened throughout: 2Y −7.2bp (3.628%), 3Y −6.7bp (3.750%), 5Y −6.2bp (3.968%), 10Y −6.1bp (4.221%), 20Y −7.7bp (4.465%) and 30Y −9.2bp (4.500%), with the ultra-long leading. Futures gained +0.19 in the 3Y (103.51) and +0.39 in the 10Y (106.33), with day ranges of 49 and 94 ticks. Volume surged to 396k and 118k contracts, 2.7x and 1.6x the prior session.

Positioning flipped outright. Going into the meeting the book was offshore long, local short; with the hike confirmed it reversed to foreigners −11,155 and securities firms +16,771. The foreign 3Y cumulative fell from a 6-month high of 133,199 to 122,044, while securities firms came off a 6-month low of −72,748 to −55,977, all in one session. Short rates priced the hike — CD91 at 3.120% (+15bp) and call at 3.040% (+24bp) — yet the 1-year MSB fell to 3.250% (−2.6bp).

The ultra-long bid came from the issuance plan. Expectations that the September funding schedule trims ultra-long supply, combined with foreign buying of ultra-long cash, drove the 30Y 9.2bp lower into the close. The maturity breakdown confirms it: beyond 10 years +3,158 was the only clearly bought bucket.

The US sold off across the curve and US–KR widened sharply. 2Y +2.1bp (4.233%), 5Y +3.8bp (4.400%), 10Y +3.0bp (4.677%), 30Y +2.6bp (5.195%). With Korea rallying alone, US–KR 10Y widened to 45.6bp (+9.1bp), taking it above the 40~43bp entry zone flagged in the weekly note.
