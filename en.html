03 Macro & Curve Dislocations

The US sold off hard over the four-day holiday. From the 09-22 close (Korea's last look) to Friday 09-25: 2Y 4.7545 → 4.8516 (+9.7bp) · 5Y +15.1bp (4.9875) · 10Y 4.9635 → 5.1604 (+19.7bp) · 30Y +19.1bp (5.4905). The 10Y traded up to 5.20% on 09-24 before retracing on Friday. The drivers were firm activity data, inflation concern and expectations of further Fed tightening, with the 2/5/7Y auctions on top.

It was a bear steepening. 2-10 went 20.9 → 30.9bp (+10.0) and 5-10 12.7 → 17.3bp (+4.6) — the belly and long end cheapened more than the front. A US curve that spent September pinned near −2σ has normalised to a 2-10 z60 of −1.10 in a week. 10-30 is roughly unchanged at 33.0bp.

Oil went the other way. Over the holiday US–Iran talks moved to a working level (Al Jazeera reported a constructive tone from the Iranian side), easing Middle East supply fears further. WTI dropped back below $100 and now trades in the low $90s. That produced some retracement in US short rates, but nowhere near all of the holiday move.

Korea, meanwhile, rallied into the break. On 09-23: 2Y −1.5 (3.965) · 3Y −3.5 (3.995) · 5Y −5.5 (4.200) · 10Y −7.7 (4.370) · 20Y −7.7 (4.453) · 30Y −7.9bp (4.550), a bull flattening, with futures 3Y +13 ticks (102.42) and 10Y +68 ticks (104.76) as offshore bought 4,731 lots of the 10Y contract. Hence this morning's gap: expected opens 3Y 102.23 (−19 ticks) and 10Y 103.81 (−95 ticks).

Even so, Korea is not following the whole move. At the expected open the 10Y contract implies about 4.52%, roughly 3bp above the 09-22 level, against +19.7bp for the UST 10Y. KR10−US10 widens from −51.6bp to about −64bp on that basis (−79bp if you simply compare the two Friday closes). At the extreme of the inversion, the KTB 10Y is hard to own outright and is better used as the sold side of a curve trade.

Today's call — the front end rather than the curve. The gap has already steepened the curve (futures 3-10 around 32bp at the open), so adding curve here is less attractive than buying the level that has cheapened. Buy the KTB 3Y and the UST 2Y, with 4.75% the floor we see on the UST 2Y. But this is not a trend change: the Fed tightening watch is alive and much of the oil retracement is already in. So add in small clips into stop-loss driven weakness rather than sizing up at once.

Flows — offshore ultra-long selling has broadened. On 09-23 offshore sold KRW 458.2bn of cash KTBs, −266.3bn of it beyond 10 years, and the notable part is that it was spread across 20–30Y off-the-runs (21-9 −21.0 · 24-10 −21.5 · 26-10 −14.0 · 24-2 −28.5 · 25-2 −75.4 · 25-7 −38.5 · 26-2 −33.5bn) rather than one benchmark. That is −1.13tn over the week. Insurers and pensions also turned small net sellers (buying 25-2 +50.0 and 26-10 +17.0bn, selling 21-2 −50.0, 20-2 −14.0 and 26-8 −49.7bn) — trimming the new 30Y they had been accumulating is a change of tone, though the size is small and it was the day before a holiday. September WGBI index flows at month-end are the offset to watch.

Policy and funding. 3Y − base fell to 99.5bp on 09-23 and goes back toward 105bp at today's open; 1Y swap − base 71.2bp. Money market: call 3.050 · CD91 3.210 · MSB 1Y 3.432%. In credit, 1Y spreads widened for a sixth straight session, but bargain hunting has started at the 9-month point (see 05).
