02 Macro & Curve Dislocations

The US was closed; Europe and Asia sold first. Treasuries sit at Friday's closes for Labor Day — 2Y 4.368, 5Y 4.548, 10Y 4.778, 30Y 5.244. On Monday Bunds +4.6bp (3.400%), gilts +4.6bp (5.106%), Australia +3.0bp (5.190%) priced the payrolls shock a day late, and WTI 92.50 (+1.11%), a seventh straight gain, has settled above $90. USD/JPY 154.27 (−1.42%) — yen strength returned — and KOSPI rose 4.61%. Tonight the US reopens and prices three days at once; higher yields at the open are the base case.

Friday's set-up still stands. After +162K payrolls the curve bear flattened with the 5Y (+3.2bp) the weakest point; only 2-5 (+1.22σ) sits above 1σ, while 5-10 (23.0, −1.21σ), 10-20 (47.0, −1.42σ) and 10-30 (46.6, −1.20σ) are all near the 3rd percentile of the past 250 days. That the 30Y fell on a strong jobs print supports the view that the long end is already sold. If Europe's +4.6bp carries over, that structure advances one more step.

Levels are at one-year highs across the curve. The 2Y at 4.368% has a 20-day z of +1.36 and a 250-day percentile of 99.2%, closing 6.8bp above the 4.30 band top for a sixth session. The 5Y at 4.548% is the highest percentile on the curve at 99.6%; the 10Y at 4.778% is inside its band (2.2bp below the 4.80 top) but also at 99.6%. Only the 30Y, at 95.6%, has stepped back. A move the size of Europe's would take the 10Y through 4.80.

This week's question is whether CPI raises or lowers the odds of a hike. The payrolls beat weakened the labour-market case for a hold, but wages did not accelerate, so the market prefers to wait for the 09/11 core print rather than price a September hike off one report. Before that come the ECB on 09/10 (deposit rate 2.25 → 2.50% hike expected) and US August PPI (+0.4% MoM, core +0.3% expected, after 0.0% in July) — if Europe hikes and PPI prints as expected, hike pricing rebuilds before CPI rather than after, and bear flattening remains the default path until then.

Crude is not backing down. With US–Iran tension persisting, WTI is up seven sessions to $92.5. After Friday's gold −1.4% and Dow −0.5%, European bonds also sold on Monday — risk assets and safe assets weak together, the signature of an inflation scare. The pressure sits in refining margins more than in crude (diesel cracks at record highs), which is the route into core rather than headline, and that bears directly on how CPI will be read.
