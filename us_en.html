View — to the levels we have had in view. The 10Y target stays 4.75; the 2Y target comes down from 4.51 to 4.37–4.41, and from there it is an unconditional take-profit. The 10Y at 4.889 is through the 60-day average (4.93) and the 5-day; the next line is the 120-day at 4.754, the same place as the target; today's range 4.81~4.9517. The 2Y at 4.582 is just above its lower Bollinger band (4.571), and the range bottom of 4.51 is the old target. The reversal trigger is a VETO or the appearance of a tool no one has thought of — the price moves first on a divided government blocking tax cuts and spending, and the moment the administration answers with a veto or an unexpected instrument is where the retracement starts. The long here is a short-term trade — a trade for the election-pricing window, so taking profit is what matters. Half is booked at the 11/4 close; the rest goes in full at target. The sequence is the BoE and the 30Y auction (11/6 03:00) → payrolls (11/6 22:30, expectations raised after JOLTS — a miss gives back more) → CPI (11/10).

Next come fiscal policy, the maturity mix and the Fed. Further tax cuts and spending are harder, so some term premium can come out, but existing supply, interest and refinancing stay, and a long budget or debt-ceiling fight shows first as specific T-bill maturities trading cheap to their neighbours. For the curve, Treasury's allocation matters more than the vote — coupon sizes were kept at the November refunding, but concentrating any increase in 2–10Y and adjusting the 20Y/30Y structure is under discussion at TBAC, and if it happens 10-30 narrows. If yields fall further, the driver is less Fed hiking priced rather than the election — softer jobs and consumption bring a bull steepening; firm data or an oil rebound takes the post-election drop back. Long end first while the political input is priced, front end once the slowdown shows in data. KR–US −93.9 (+2.65) — near the 60-day low of −99, watched, no entry.
