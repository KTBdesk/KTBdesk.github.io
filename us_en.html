02 Macro & Curve Dislocations

Crude cleared USD 90 and the whole curve sold off. On 09-01 2Y +5.4bp (4.397%), 5Y +5.7bp (4.557%), 10Y +4.7bp (4.799%), 20Y +3.5bp (5.278%) and 30Y +3.2bp (5.275%) — a bear flattening led by the front end, the inverse of the prior session. A Saudi tanker was struck leaving the Strait of Hormuz and the US carried out further strikes on Iran, reviving closure and supply-disruption fears: WTI 90.22 (+5.20%), up 9.9% over three sessions.

The data pointed the same way. ISM manufacturing prices did not climb further but held at an elevated level, and the employment component stayed firm inside a low-hiring, low-firing mix. Inflation that will not fall while employment refuses to break gives the market nothing with which to unwind the September hike now in the price.

Today's RBNZ is about the language, not the number. A hike is expected unanimously, so the tone of the accompanying comments is what matters rather than the decision itself. Japan is in the same position — a September hike is already fully in the price — so even hawkish remarks have little room to move the market. With the major hiking cycles now priced across the board, global front ends cannot extend without genuinely new information, which is the same logic behind the US 2Y stalling.

Levels across the curve are at 250-day highs. On a 20-day z, 5Y +2.35σ, 10Y +2.32σ and 2Y +2.10σ — all three at the 100th percentile of the past 250 days. Outright levels, not curves, now dominate the anomaly screen; what remains on the curve side is the 2-5-10 butterfly (8.25bp, −2.13σ, 0.4th percentile) and 10-30 (47.6bp, −1.71σ, 6th percentile).

The 10Y has reached the top of the band. At 4.799% it sits 0.2bp from the 4.80 band top, testing it ahead of the 09/04 payrolls and the 09/11 CPI. While crude holds in the 90s, term premium can keep pushing that top higher. On the curve, 5-10 (24.2bp, −0.90σ) and 10-20 (47.9bp, −1.17σ) remain below 1σ, so the steepener side still holds the opportunity.
