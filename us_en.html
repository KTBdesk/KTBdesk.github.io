03 Macro & Curve Dislocations

The 30Y auction cleared just as the 10Y had. Across the two sessions Korea missed: 2Y +2.2 (4.790) · 5Y −1.0 (5.0175) · 10Y −4.4 (5.241) · 20Y −6.6 (5.6455) · 30Y −7.5bp (5.5975) — a textbook bull flattening, with the biggest falls furthest out. The 10Y had already stopped at 5.30%, through the market, with a 2.77x cover; the 30Y was then taken down too. Absorbing supply is where the long end gets covered — confirmed twice in one week.

And the curve genuinely unwound. 2-10 went from 53.2bp intraday on 10/8 to 45.1, eight basis points flatter. It could not reach its 60-day high of 53.35 and turned, which says much of the steepener built from the 60-day low of 20.3 has now been cleared out. 10-30 followed, 38.8 to 35.65bp, and 10-20 42.75 to 40.45. Trades positioned for long-end short covering did their job this week.

The front went the other way. The 2Y rose 2.2bp to 4.790%, the only tenor up. The September minutes, with most participants seeing one more hike by year-end as likely appropriate, were being chewed over again, and Michigan one-year inflation expectations rising from 4.6% to 4.7% pointed the same way even as sentiment missed at 46.3 against 47.8. October hike odds are 19%. That took 2-5 to 22.75bp, 3.1bp off its 60-day high of 25.85, while the 2-5-10 fly at −0.40 is still near its 60-day low of −0.75, one-year z −2.54 — the 5Y remains the cheap point.

This week is all about inflation. CPI 10/14 (3.4% headline, 2.4% core expected), PPI 10/15, euro area CPI 10/16. The thing to watch is oil: WTI is back to 91.85 from 88.28, so the IEA's brought-forward releases and diesel priority faded within three sessions — the same direction as the ISM prices-paid story. Curve trades have already moved once this week, so this is not the moment to put them on fresh; keep the relative-value view that the 5Y is cheap and leave direction until Wednesday.
