02 Macro & Curve Dislocations

Waller opened the door to a September hold. Governor Waller said he could argue for holding at the September FOMC if coming data confirm inflation pressure is easing, and the implied probability of a hike fell from around 63% to roughly 50%. It is the first time the hawkish pricing has run in the other direction since Jackson Hole.

The rates response was still muted. On 09-03 2Y −3.1bp (4.339%), 5Y −1.9bp (4.516%), 10Y −1.1bp (4.769%), 20Y −1.5bp (5.249%) and 30Y −1.0bp (5.248%). A 13-point drop in hike probability moved the 2Y just 3.1bp. Across two sessions that is −6.2bp, barely a third of the +16.7bp put on over 8/28 and 9/1.

The yen made a large move. USD/JPY fell 1.98% to 156.65, more than three yen in a day. It is the same shape as the bull steepening that followed the early-August intervention, though intervention here is presumed, not confirmed. Gilts rallied hard as well, with the UK 10Y down 9.6bp to 5.065%, and Bunds fell 3.1bp.

Crude is up for a fifth session. WTI 91.30 (+0.32%). The pace has slowed noticeably but the direction has not, so the floor under long-end yields stays in place. On the curve, 2-5 at 17.6bp and +1.18σ is the only spread above 1σ — the front end is already wide internally — while 10-20 (48.0bp, −1.13σ) and 10-30 (47.9bp, −0.84σ) remain compressed, so the steepener opportunity sits further out.

Tonight's payrolls set the direction. With hike odds back near 50%, a firm print reverses that repricing just as fast as it arrived. That is the direct consequence of the labour market rejoining inflation as something the Fed has said it is watching.
