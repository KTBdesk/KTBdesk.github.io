02 Macro & Curve Dislocations

Payrolls were strong and the curve bear flattened. August nonfarm payrolls printed +162K, three times the mid-50K consensus, with unemployment at 4.1% and the prior two months revised up by a combined +55K — the first positive print in five months. The 2Y spiked to around 4.40% intraday before settling at 4.368% (+2.9bp); 5Y +3.2bp (4.548%), 10Y +1.0bp (4.778%), 20Y −0.1bp (5.248%), 30Y −0.4bp (5.244%). The front end sold off and the long end actually rallied — exactly the "firm print, bear flattening" path set out in Friday's preview. The 4.274% level flagged for a 2Y short was never approached.

The 5Y took the most damage. With 5Y +3.2bp the weakest point, only 2-5 widened (+0.4bp to 18.0, +1.22σ); everything beyond it compressed — 5-10 −2.3bp (23.0, −1.21σ), 10-20 −1.0bp (47.0, −1.42σ), 10-30 −1.4bp (46.6, −1.20σ). All three sit at roughly the 3rd percentile of the past 250 days. That the 30Y fell on a strong payrolls print, with long-end spreads already this compressed, adds one more day of evidence that the long end is already sold.

Levels are at one-year highs across the curve. The 2Y at 4.368% carries a 20-day z of +1.53 and a 250-day percentile of 99.2%, and has now closed above the 4.30 band top for six sessions (6.8bp above). The 5Y at 4.548% is the highest percentile on the curve at 99.6%; the 10Y at 4.778% is inside its band (2.2bp below the 4.80 top) but also at 99.2%. Only the 30Y, at 95.6%, has stepped back from the top.

This week's question is whether CPI raises or lowers the odds of a hike. The payrolls beat weakened the case for holding on the grounds of a softening labour market. Wages did not accelerate alongside, however, so the market is more inclined to wait for the 09/11 core print than to price a September hike off one jobs report. If core does not slow convincingly, hike pricing can rebuild quickly — and until then, bear flattening is the default path for the curve. Before that come the ECB on 09/10 (deposit rate 2.25 → 2.50% hike expected) and US August PPI (+0.4% MoM, core +0.3% expected, after 0.0% in July) — if Europe hikes and PPI prints as expected, hike pricing rebuilds before CPI rather than after.

Crude is not backing down. With US–Iran military tension rising again, WTI settled at 91.48 (+0.20%), a sixth straight gain, holding above $90. Risk assets took the jobs data badly (gold −1.4%, Dow −0.5%). Bunds (3.354%, −0.5bp) and gilts (5.060%, −0.5bp) edged firmer, diverging from Treasuries; USD/JPY was flat at 156.49.
