03 Macro & Curve Dislocations

Equities bounced and bonds rallied anyway — led by the front end. Friday's cash KTBs 2Y −2.5 (3.805) · 3Y −3.7 (3.835) · 5Y −3.9 (3.970) · 10Y −3.0 (4.165) · 20Y −4.2 (4.198) · 30Y −4.0 (4.257) · 50Y −11.3 (4.162). Futures 3Y +10 ticks (103.05) · 10Y +34 (106.64). Yields fell through KOSPI +2.05% (5,668) and KOSDAQ +4.46% because offshore bought 13,683 3Y and 6,283 10Y contracts and poured offers into IRS — bond-swap 3Y −0.5 (z −2.03) and 2Y −4.25, swaps outperforming cash. 2-3 3.0 · 2-5 16.5 · 3-5 13.5 · 3-30 42.2 · futures 3-10 26.8 are all 60-day lows, 3-10 33.0 (+0.7), 10-30 9.2 (−1.0), and 30-50 −9.5 (−7.3) is the 50Y rallying after its auction. 3Y 83.5bp over base (−3.7). Friday's "bear steepening if equities hold" was wrong — offshore bought duration regardless of equities.

The nature of the offshore bid — fear of not owning it. Market talk has hedge funds looking at Korea's AI and semiconductor exposure as a reason to own Korean duration, and adding it first through IRS and futures, which tie up less cash. With CTAs buying on technical signals, the read is that a "reluctant rally" can keep going. The same accounts, though, put front-end downside at about 20bp if the terminal rate is 3.50–3.75 — the 3Y at 3.835 is 83.5bp over a 3.00 base, 58bp once a November hike to 3.25 is counted. A front-end-led move that has pushed 2-3 to 3.0 also means the short end gets relatively weaker as the November hike approaches. The Philadelphia semiconductor index fell for an eighth straight day (first since 2008); Nasdaq −2.14%.

After the 50Y auction — insurers took 26-11, and the 26-8 short is rebuilding. KRW 600bn of 26-11 cleared at 4.195% (8bp through the mid) on a 246% bid-to-cover, the 50Y rallied 11.3bp and 30-50 went to −9.5. Insurers and pensions took over 30% of the 50Y auction (26-11 +204bn) plus 25-7 +70 and 26-8 +36, and with STRIPS +370bn (26-11 principal +260) bought about KRW 607bn of post-2040 assets — the largest real-money print on an auction day. Even so, excluding auction days their October ultra-long buying is still net selling. Offshore bought 26-8 +65 and 26-11 +60 while selling 25-7 −110, 25-9 −60 and 24-10 −35, net −110bn in the ultra-long — 26-8 is +767bn in October excluding the auction day, but the off-the-runs keep being sold. In the 5Y, 26-9 +70 against 26-3 −150 and 25-3 −50, selling outweighing the switch. Borrow: 26-8 +24bn (1,410bn, 18.33%) rebuilding after Thursday's −99, 26-5 −332bn (1,761bn, 9.83%), −1,958bn on the week — the auction-line short is done. 26-6 −1bn (1,937bn): no short built into today's KRW 2.1tn auction, which reads as accounts wanting the option after the auction — leave room for a strong result.

The US rallied only at the front. 10/16 2Y 4.709 (−3.0) · 5Y 4.8685 (−0.9) · 10Y 5.075 (+1.45) · 30Y 5.4225 (+1.55), 2-10 36.6 (+4.45), a bull steepening. After Thursday's −12.7 the long end paused while more hike pricing came out of the front. US equities: S&P −1.1%, Nasdaq −2.14%, Micron and Intel down more than 5% — doubts about AI capex durability and funding costs at high rates, with reports of Anthropic's IPO valuation being cut and a 10% yield on its financing adding to the mood. Warsh told the IMF meetings in Bangkok (10/16) that "AI is obviously hitting the demand side of the economy before the supply side" and that central bankers should treat it as a shock — the same line as Governor Shin's remark (10/13) on a possibly higher neutral rate. Equities are selling off on AI worries while central banks read AI as demand-side inflation pressure. The worst case for bonds is growth forecasts being revised up at Q3 GDP (10/28) and the BOK's November update. WTI 101.41 (+3.5%) and Brent near 110 — Middle East supply worries plus strikes on Russian refineries. KR10−US10 −91 (−4.5).

Strategy — offshore flow, not risk assets, is setting rates now. ① The 10Y auction (KRW 2.1tn, 10:40) can clear strongly if option demand meets the global rally. No chasing the 10Y contract before it; direction after the result. ② 2-3 at 3.0 and 3-5 at 13.5 are 60-day lows — the 3Y stays the strongest tenor while offshore keeps buying 3Y futures and IRS, but 2Y vs 3Y is a retracement candidate as the November hike approaches. Keep the 3Y futures long into the BOK (10/22) but bring forward the switch into the 1–2Y. ③ 10-30 9.2: no auction pullback came; the 5~6bp entry rule stands. ④ Credit keeps underperforming on bank issuance — if credit cannot support a KTB rally, the rally's durability is short. No added exposure. ⑤ Keep buy 26-5 / sell 26-3 at 15.5 (z −2.51); the 26-5 short is fully repaid.
