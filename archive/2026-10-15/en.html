Real cash demand exists only in the 30Y. Insurers bought KRW 121bn beyond 10 years (25-7 +50bn, 26-2 +16bn, some STRIPS) — small, but the first insurer/pension ultra-long buying since September — and offshore took KRW 101bn of the 30Y benchmark 26-8 (the rest of the ultra-long −34bn). On the week insurers are +875bn beyond 10 years and offshore +805bn. The front end is the opposite: asset managers −195bn under one year, offshore −380bn in 1–2Y on the week, the MSB auction tailed the benchmark, and 1–2Y credit spreads are widening across banks, agencies and finance companies (only 3Y quality paper holds at benchmark levels). Of offshore's +678bn in cash KTBs, 70% is sub-one-year (22-1 +150, 25-1 +80, 22-8 +60, 17-3 +50) and the 5Y line is a 26-9 +112 / 26-3 −149 benchmark switch — no directional buying in the belly. So if equities fall, the intraday long is in the 10Y, and the pressure into the close is a steeper curve — the only cash bid underneath is in the 30Y and the front end is sitting under supply (MSBs, bank paper), so a 10Y-led session gives back by the close.
