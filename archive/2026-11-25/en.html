View — ahead of the BOK, the pressure is towards a firmer market. Narrower gasoline and diesel cracks, downward pressure on energy prices and the slide in copper are all known news, and there is doubt over whether they last. Attacks on ships are rising again and freight rates have rebounded after their collapse, so worries of inflation re-igniting remain. Even so, in countries that hiked pre-emptively, such as Korea and Australia, demand from institutions that have not been able to buy and early demand for new-year money are in support. Australia's CPI today can be read as a peak, with downward pressure to follow, even if the headline is high. → Pressure towards a firmer market. BOK 11/26 — the market leans towards a 25bp hike; watch the dot plot, dissent and growth. At the ends of the dot plot the desk looks for 0–2 dots at 3.00% and 0–2 at 3.75%, and about one dissent for a hold. Late in the hiking cycle, the question is whether a signal of the end appears or pressure for more hikes next year is left in place. The rally peaks at the BOK meeting where a fall in chip prices has actually arrived and is written into the growth forecast — it takes a quarter or two for lower prices to show in export unit values and quarterly growth, so that is judged to be February next year at the earliest and more likely the May forecast round. → Until then, buyers on the sidelines have the upper hand.
