02 Macro & Curve Dislocations

Payrolls were strong, and the response was the bear flattening set out in Friday's preview. August nonfarm payrolls printed +162K, three times the mid-50K consensus, with unemployment at 4.1% and the prior two months revised up by a combined +55K — the first positive print in five months. Treasuries: 2Y +2.9bp (4.368%), 5Y +3.2bp (4.548%), 10Y +1.0bp (4.778%), 30Y −0.4bp (5.244%) — the front end sold off while the long end actually rallied. The 2Y spiked to around 4.40% intraday before settling at 4.368%. The "firm print, bear flattening" path played out exactly, and the 4.274% level flagged for a 2Y short was never approached. Wages did not accelerate alongside, so the market is more inclined to wait for the 09/11 CPI than to price a September hike off one report — and until then bear-flattening pressure on the US curve is the default.

Korea on Friday: the 10Y led, both ends stood still. 5Y −1.3bp (4.095%), 10Y −1.7bp (4.350%), 20Y −2.0bp (4.570%) rallied, while 3Y +0.3bp (3.885%) and 30Y unchanged (4.637%) did not move. Futures told the same story: 10Y +24 ticks (105.44) vs 3Y +3 ticks (103.17). Result: 3-10 −2.0bp (46.5) narrower, and only the very back widened — 10-30 +1.7bp (28.7), 20-30 +2.0bp (6.7) — leaving 20-30 at +1.03σ as the only spread above 1σ.

Offshore sold the 3Y contract again after one day, and the 10Y cumulative position has overtaken the 3Y. Futures: 3Y −4,732, 10Y +4,068. Cumulative 10Y at 58,825 now exceeds cumulative 3Y at 50,632 — on 8/26 the 3Y stood at 133,199, more than double the 10Y, and that gap has inverted in nine sessions. In cash, offshore sold KRW 505.8bn of KTBs, a second day of selling and the largest since month-end.

But the cash selling is of mixed character. The two largest sales, 17-7 and 24-12 (both maturing 2027.12, 1.3y remaining), KRW 351.5bn combined, make up 60% of the total — a continuation of the stop-loss selling in short paper seen recently. In the ultra-long sector offshore bought 26-8 (30.0y, the new 30Y benchmark) +114.0bn and 21-2 +31.0bn but sold 26-2 (29.5y) −114.9bn, 25-9 −40.8bn, 24-2 −20.4bn and others, net sellers. The 26-2 sale coincided with a similar-sized bank purchase, so a bond-forward unwind is a live possibility and we do not read it as pure direction. What is clear is that fresh ultra-long buying has slowed markedly since the month-end WGBI inflow, and insurers and pension funds (26-2 +168.4bn, 26-8 +125.4bn) are filling that space.

Three weights sit on the front end today. ① the KTB 3Y 26-5 auction, KRW 3.2tn (10:40); ② US 2Y +2.9bp; ③ 26-5 securities-lending balance +357.0bn (30,565, 26.6% of the issue), a sharp rise the day before the auction. The 10Y contract, meanwhile, carries Friday's +24 ticks plus an 8-tick premium to fair value with six sessions to expiry, so the gap down could exceed what cash implies. Expected opens: 3Y 103.10 (−7 ticks), 10Y 105.21 (−23 ticks). If front-end pressure persists into CPI (09/11), Korea also leans toward flattening.
