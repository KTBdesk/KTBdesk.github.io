03 Macro & Curve Dislocations

The buyer changed. KTB 2Y −5.4 (3.976) · 3Y −5.7 (4.015) · 5Y −5.3 (4.211) · 10Y −5.5 (4.415) · 20Y −7.2 (4.438) · 30Y −6.2 (4.530) · 50Y −6.3bp (4.495), futures 3Y +18 ticks (102.37) · 10Y +58 ticks (104.44). Where 09-29 was short covering in futures, 09-30 was offshore buying cash outright: KRW 2.06tn of KTBs, of which 722.3bn beyond 10 years. Foreign KRW bond holdings rose KRW 2.0tn in a day to 345.9tn. The month-end WGBI index flow we flagged as the thing to watch actually showed up.

Domestic accounts split. Banks bought KRW 975.1bn (ultra-long +336.9bn) and investment trusts 267.1bn, while insurers sold 364.7bn (ultra-long −255.2bn) and pensions 283.4bn. The two that dumped the ultra-long the day before kept selling, and offshore absorbed all of it and more. On the week (09/22–09/30) offshore KTB buying is still only −78.9bn, so one session nearly undid a week of selling.

In futures, though, offshore sold the 10Y. 3Y +4,054 lots / 10Y −2,096, with securities (+3,443) on the other side of the 10Y. Even so the contract gained 58 ticks and its 9.2-tick discount disappeared entirely, flipping to 2.1 ticks rich, while the 3Y stayed 4.3 ticks cheap. There is no longer a cheap way to own back-end delta through futures.

The US sold off the same night. 2Y +0.95bp (4.887) · 5Y +3.64 (5.0859) · 10Y +4.84 (5.2849) · 20Y +5.03 (5.6713) · 30Y +6.11bp (5.6286). On the day August PCE printed, the 2Y did not move and the damage grew with maturity — term premium and supply, not a repricing of policy. The 2-5-10 fly closed at 0.0bp (60-day −1.98σ, one-year −2.70σ), another low: the UST 5Y is the cheapest point on the curve.

Every cross-market spread made a new low.
· KR10−US10 −87.0bp — 10.3bp wider on the day, 60-day z −3.71 (average −40.6), a 60-day low
· KR30−US10 −75.5bp — 11.0bp wider, 60-day z −3.61 (average −16.4), a 60-day low
· KR20−US10 −84.7bp (z −3.42) · KR3−US2 −87.2bp (z −2.70)
→ On a one-year window, though, KR10 is only −1.10σ and KR30 −0.44σ, with lows of −121 and −133. Extreme on 60 days, mid-range on a year — both need to be held in view.

Korea's curve is still compressed, and only the 20Y got richer. 2-5 23.5bp (−2.40σ) · 2-3 3.9 (−2.15σ) · 3-10 40.0 (−2.06σ) at the front and belly, against 20-30 9.2bp (60-day +1.10σ, one-year +2.12σ) and a 10-20-30 fly of 6.9 (60-day +1.82σ, one-year +2.38σ), with 26-10 the strongest bond on the curve at −7.2bp. But 10-20 at 2.3bp still carries the 09-10 benchmark switch (25-9 → 26-10): measured against the old benchmark 25-9 (4.517) the same spread is 10.2bp, so the z overstates it. With 26-10 only KRW 842bn listed and 34.7% of it on loan, shorting it is not practical, so we leave a 20Y short out of the recommendations.

Today's issuance plan sets the back end. At end-September 74.90% of the 2026 programme had been issued — effectively exactly on schedule — and by tenor the running totals are 10Y +21.5%, 20Y +15.6% and 30Y +9.4% ahead of plan, with only the 50Y behind at −0.1%. Arithmetically there is little room to add ultra-long supply and some room to add 50Y. A neutral-to-lighter plan alongside continued offshore buying would extend yesterday's move. Money market: call 3.120 (+1.0) · CD91 3.220 · MSB 1Y 3.539% (+1.1); MMF balances fell KRW 6.01tn into quarter end.
