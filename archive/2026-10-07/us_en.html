03 Macro & Curve Dislocations

Overnight strength came from Europe, not from anything American. France put forward a tougher fiscal consolidation than the market expected, French government yields fell sharply, and the move travelled through the euro area into Treasuries. Oil holding flat (WTI 89.44) stopped inflation worries from building further. 2Y 4.800 (−1.4) · 5Y 5.036 (−2.3) · 10Y 5.284 (−2.2) · 30Y 5.661 (−0.3), with the 10Y trading roughly 3bp below its Asian-session level. German 10Y 3.497 (−0.9), UK 5.381 (−4.8), Australia 5.379 (−3.0).

And again only the front came down. 2-10 compressed 0.95bp while 10-30 widened 1.95 and 10-20 1.45, leaving the 20Y at 5.702% and the 30Y at 5.660% — both series highs (one-year z +3.16 and +3.22). That a fiscal tightening in Europe was the good news is itself the diagnosis of the US back end — US fiscal policy is not tightening, so a European-led rally reached the front and never got to 20 and 30 years. 10-30 at 37.75bp carries z60 −1.15 and one-year z −2.23: outright levels are at one-year highs while the curve still has not steepened.

The most compressed segment is 2-5. At 23.65bp it is a 60-day high (z +2.34), and the 2-5-10 fly at 1.05bp (z60 −1.56, one-year −2.44) has come 0.75bp off its one-year low. That the 2Y barely moved from 4.8% through a collapsing payrolls number says the front is already expensive enough; if a retracement comes, the 5Y is where it shows up first.

Everything left this week is supply. Tonight the 10Y auction (02:00, 4.834% last) and FOMC minutes (03:00), then the 30Y on 10/9 (5.308% last). With every crowded curve trade short the long end, absorbing that supply is where long-end short covering tends to happen. CPI (10/14) and PPI (10/15) are the next fork. Korea is closed on 10/9 for Hangul Day, so the 30Y auction only gets priced on 10/12 — cut Korean exposure before the 10/8 close.
