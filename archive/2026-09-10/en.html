02 Macro & Curve Dislocations

Treasuries set fresh one-year highs across the curve the day before PPI. 2Y +3.8bp (4.434%), 5Y +5.4bp (4.618%), 10Y +5.2bp (4.843%), 20Y +3.9bp (5.292%), 30Y +4.5bp (5.291%) — the 250-day percentile is 100% at every point. The belly took the most damage, pushing 2-5 to 18.6bp (+1.65), +1.24σ, back above 1σ, while the back stayed compressed: 10-20 at 44.95bp is the 0.4th percentile of 250 days. Europe sold with it — Bunds +7.9bp (3.459%), gilts +9.3bp (5.199%). Intraday the 10Y traded through 4.85% and the 30Y through 5.29%, and the move carried disappointment with the Treasury buyback alongside crude.

Crude did the pushing, and geopolitics did the pushing of crude. Iran said it fired ballistic missiles at a US base in Jordan, and both the US and Iran reported vessel attacks in Middle East waters, reviving supply-disruption fears. WTI settled 96.05 (+3.25%), a ninth straight gain, and Brent 100.71 (+2.85%) — its first triple-digit print since July. President Trump's remark that the conflict with Iran could run past the midterms was read as extending the risk premium rather than capping it. With PPI consensus already high at +0.4% MoM (+0.3% core), the market moved ahead of the data.

Korea could not find a direction and slipped in the afternoon. There was some early strength, but the 2–5Y sector sold off again after lunch and even 10Y+ gave back its gains to flat — despite crude rising through the session.

The result was a bear flattening: the front end is cheap, the 20Y is expensive. 3Y +1.2bp (3.910%), 2Y +1.1bp (3.726%) and 5Y +0.6bp (4.133%) sold off while the 10Y was unchanged (4.400%) and the 20Y rallied 0.9bp (4.552%). So 3-10 −1.2bp (49.0), 5-10 −0.6bp (26.7) and 10-20 −0.9bp (15.2) all narrowed. 10-20 at 15.2bp is a 5- and 60-day low, while 20-30 widened 1.1bp to 9.0, +1.58σ and the 98.4th percentile — now the widest point on the curve.

Four benchmarks roll today — and the premium is already building. 2Y 26-1→26-7, 5Y 26-3→04125-310, 20Y 25-9→26-10, 30Y 26-2→26-8. Yesterday the incoming 20Y 26-10 traded at 4.525%, 2.7bp through the outgoing 25-9 (4.552%), and the incoming 2Y 26-7 outperformed 25-4 by 1.1bp in a single day (spread z from 0.00 to +0.60). The new lines richening and the old ones cheapening was already underway the day before the switch.

Offshore cash was flat in cash terms (−KRW 8.1bn) but the composition flipped. ≤1Y at −401.5bn explains the entire sale (1Y MSB −200.0bn, FX-stabilisation paper −368.0bn), while ≤10Y +224.8bn and >10Y +171.4bn turned to buying. By issue, they bought the 10Y benchmark 26-6 at +191.7bn — a reversal from −39.0bn the day before — and took both 20Y lines (25-9 +109.0bn, 26-10 +69.0bn). Insurers added +311.1bn of KTBs (>10Y +227.2bn) and banks +450.8bn. That is why the 10Y held flat. In futures, though, offshore sold heavily: 3Y −9,952, 10Y −2,141 after two days of buying.

The concentration in the 20Y is worth watching separately. Market talk through July and August attributed the steady 25-9 buying to an overseas sovereign-wealth-type account, so if 25-9 buying continues at size for several sessions, that demand may be returning. But the ultra-long has been alternating between net buying and net selling day to day (−160bn the prior session, +171.4bn yesterday), so one day settles nothing. Domestically, insurers and pension funds took 26-10 +150.0bn, 26-2 +62.0bn and 26-8 +14.0bn — over KRW 210bn in the ultra-long, holding the stance they have run since late August, and the banks' selling of 26-6 and 25-9 largely mirrors offshore's buying, so it may be order-handling flow rather than direction.

Geopolitical risk is being read as inflation re-acceleration, not as a flight to safety. In this regime a crude spike transmits through higher breakevens into a wider term premium, which means US long yields cannot fall easily — and if they cannot, Korea's 10Y and beyond will struggle to break lower on anything short of a major catalyst. Views on the Fed are split too: a Reuters poll has most economists expecting a September hold, but after firm payrolls and the crude move, more are allowing for a resumption of hikes.

Today — the place to shift from the curve to delta. Flattening on sell-offs has worked, but US 2-10 is meeting firm resistance around 38bp and the 10Y auction was well absorbed. With PPI consensus already set high, we lean toward a strong retracement. On crude, Brent should meet heavy resistance around $103.8, so if you want length, the entry is a 2Y long once that level is reached. If you have been running the curve as previously suggested, cover the hedge and move to a delta play. The reason to keep the length at the front is that the term premium keeps the back end heavy, while the front has more room to retrace once inflation is confirmed.
