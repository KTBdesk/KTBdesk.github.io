03 Macro & Curve Dislocations

Payrolls collapsed and only long yields went up. US September payrolls came in at +29k against 90k expected, unemployment rose to 4.2% and prior months were revised down, taking the odds of an October hike from a peak of 70% to around 20%. Yet the UST 10Y is 5.306%, the 20Y 5.711% and the 30Y 5.664% — all the highest since 2002 (one-year z of +3.26, +3.26 and +3.30), while the 2Y sat still at 4.813%. The front end is priced off monetary policy and the long end off the deficit, supply and term premium — the two have separated. Over the two sessions 10/1 to 10/5: 2Y +2.1 · 5Y +4.6 · 10Y +6.4 · 20Y +5.9 · 30Y +4.9bp.

Korea rallied across the board on 10/2, and only the 30Y lagged. KTB 2Y −7.9 (3.886) · 3Y −8.8 (3.927) · 5Y −8.5 (4.115) · 10Y −8.5 (4.357) · 20Y −6.5 (4.375) · 30Y −5.0bp (4.502), futures 3Y +28 ticks (102.64) · 10Y +72 ticks (104.84). That took 10-30 from 11.0 to 14.5bp and 20-30 to 12.7bp (one-year z +2.57). The 3Y is now 92.7bp over the base rate, back under 100bp (101.5 on 10/1). Futures stay cheap at 4.6 and 6.6 ticks.

The curve is where the crowd sits. Cross-market spreads made new 60-day lows again — KR10−US10 −94.9bp (60-day z −3.51), KR30−US10 −80.4 and KR20−US10 −93.1 — so owning the KTB 10Y outright takes the full hit when the US sells off. The bigger point is positioning. US 2-10 at 49.3bp has come right up under its 60-day high of 53.4, and steepeners put on from the 60-day low of 20.3 start to look like unwind candidates above 50bp. Because every one of those trades is short the long end, a bearish catalyst can produce short covering at the back rather than more steepening — and the 10Y auction on 10/8 and the 30Y on 10/9 look like the best moments for it.

At the ultra-long the problem is demand, not supply. October issuance was cut, but offshore is not buying the long end: on 10/2 they bought KRW 111.3bn of the 10Y benchmark 26-6 while selling 21-2 (−85.0bn), 23-2 (−40.9bn) and 26-2 (−20.0bn) against purchases of 26-8 (+47.0bn) and 24-2 (+40.2bn). Insurers and pensions actually sold the new 30Y benchmark 26-8 (−18.7bn) and are visibly weaker than in September. Most of all, the WGBI passive inflow ends this month and no replacement 20–30Y demand has shown up. Lending sits at 40.4% of 26-8 and 41.8% of 26-10, so there is no room left to short either. Tomorrow's KRW 1.8tn 30Y auction is the first real test.

On 10-02 the KTB rally was large enough that bank paper beyond 1.5 years traded through to slightly cheap of the KTB curve, and 3-year commercial-bank names printed about 1bp cheap to the 3Y benchmark. Agency paper at two years traded 5bp through its own reference but still 1–2bp cheap to KTBs, and in card and capital names the nine-month to one-year bucket traded 2–3bp through while buyers beyond 1.5 years stayed passive. → Local accounts now treat the sub-one-year bucket as high absolute yield and carry without the duration. The spread to 1.5–2 years only closes once KTB volatility settles — and that is when the wider spreads and roll-down there become worth taking selectively.
